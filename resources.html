The people whose work this is built on

Crop Circle Watch photographs nothing, measures nothing, and investigates nothing. Every formation on this site was found, flown, photographed, surveyed, argued over and written up by somebody else — very often for free, on their own time, for decades. This page is where they get named.

Several of the people below have been documenting this subject since before the web existed, and have kept doing it through long stretches when almost nobody was paying attention. That sustained, unglamorous record-keeping is the entire reason a dashboard like this one can exist at all: a daily scan is a trivial thing to build on top of thirty-five years of somebody else's fieldwork. We are grateful for it, and the least we can do is send people back to the source. Every entry on this site links to whoever reported it. None of this is a substitute for visiting them directly. Please do.

The two at the centre of the field

Between them, these two have carried the public record of this phenomenon for thirty years. Most entries on this dashboard cite one or both, and would not exist without them.

Aggregator cropcircleconnector.com

The Crop Circle Connector

The field's central clearing house, and the closest thing this subject has to an institution — maintained continuously since the 1990s. A season index for every year, with a page per formation carrying aerial photography, an OS grid reference, and often ground reports, diagrams and dowsing notes. They also had the foresight to put the report year in every URL, which is a small kindness to anyone building on top of them: it is why this dashboard can tell a live report from an old page resurfacing in search.

  • Season index by year
  • Map references
  • Aerial photo sets
  • Ground reports

With thanks for three decades of unbroken season coverage. Usually the first to publish a new formation, and the backbone of this archive.

Aggregator temporarytemples.co.uk

Temporary Temples

The life's work of Steve and Karen Alexander, who have been photographing this phenomenon since 1994. Every formation gets a project page built around their own aerial photography, with geometric drawings, measurements and a careful discussion of how the pattern is constructed. Alongside that: books and year books, a video library, online courses and an annual conference. The care in their write-ups sets the standard this project measures itself against.

  • Formation analysis
  • Geometry drawings
  • Year books
  • Conference & courses

With thanks for the most considered write-up any formation receives. Typically a few days behind the first report, and always worth the wait.

The people in the fields and in the air

Someone has to get up at dawn, get permission, and get the shot before the crop is cut. Almost every image and every video on this dashboard exists because one of them did.

Photography cropcirclephotographs.co.uk

Lucy Pringle

A photographer and researcher who has been documenting formations since 1990 — one of the longest continuous visual archives anyone in this field has assembled, and the product of thirty-five consecutive seasons of turning up. Her site carries that archive alongside articles and newsletters, and she runs guided tours of the Wiltshire formations and the ancient sites around them.

  • Archive from 1990
  • Articles & newsletter
  • Guided tours

With thanks for the long view. Thirty-five years of continuous photographic record, kept when nobody was asking her to.

Video youtube.com/@StonehengeDronescapes

Stonehenge Dronescapes

A drone pilot who flies the Wiltshire formations between Avebury and Stonehenge through the season, publishing short aerial films — often within a day or two of a formation appearing, which given how quickly these things are harvested is the difference between a record and no record. A large share of the footage embedded on this dashboard is theirs, and is embedded so that views go to their channel.

  • Aerial video
  • Same-week uploads
  • Wiltshire focus

With thanks for letting us see the pattern whole. Frequently the only aerial record a formation gets before it is cut.

Archivists, sceptics and the community

The people keeping the historical record from disappearing, the ones willing to say a formation is human-made, and the open forums where word travels first. A field needs all three, and this one is lucky to have them.

Archive circleresearcharchive.com

Crop Circle Research Archive

A free digital repository patiently digitising the paper archives of researchers active from the 1980s through the early 2000s — over 600 magazines and articles so far, plus chronologies and bibliographies, and still being scanned. Rescuing a paper record before it rots is slow, thankless work that nobody gets credit for, and it is the only reason the early decades of this subject are legible at all.

  • 600+ digitised magazines
  • Chronologies
  • Bibliographies

With thanks for saving the pre-internet record. Material that would otherwise exist nowhere online, and eventually nowhere at all.

Commentary thecroppie.com

The Croppie

A blog covering the British scene since 2015, and the field's most sceptical regular voice. Alongside formation write-ups it runs commentary columns, a reader Q&A with working circle makers, and patient debunkings of claims circulating in the community. Being the person who says "no, that one was made by people, and here is how" is not a popular job in this subject, and doing it consistently for a decade is a real service to it.

  • Season commentary
  • Circle-maker interviews
  • Sceptical analysis

With thanks for the scrutiny. The source most likely to establish that a formation is human-made, and to show its working.

Community reddit.com/r/cropcircles

r/cropcircles

The main open discussion venue, and often where word of a new formation travels before the aggregators publish — a genuinely useful early signal, contributed by people with no reason to bother other than interest. What it cannot offer is date structure: posts carry none, and reposted photo sets of old formations are common. That is a property of the format, not a criticism of the posters, and this dashboard treats a thread as a lead to verify elsewhere rather than as a citation.

  • Early word-of-mouth
  • Photo sets
  • Open discussion

With thanks for the early word, freely given. The first place a formation is usually mentioned by anyone at all.

Crop Circles and More

Years of season coverage set alongside the ancient sites the formations appear near — a smaller, quieter operation than the two primary aggregators, run by someone doing it purely out of interest in the subject. Owed thanks, currently unreachable

Work this field is indebted to, from a site that did not resolve when this page was last checked. Named here without a link rather than left out — the contribution stands whether or not the domain is up.

  • BLT Research bltresearch.com — Nancy Talbott's team, whose laboratory work on plant and soil abnormalities at formation sites, some of it published in peer-reviewed journals, is the most rigorous physical-evidence effort anyone has made here. The "unexplained (anomaly evidence)" verdicts in our research index largely rest on their findings. The domain did not resolve at time of writing, checked against public DNS as well as local.

If we have got something wrong

  • This dashboard aggregates automatically, and an automated process can misattribute, over-quote, or link to the wrong page. If you are one of the people above and something here mis-credits your work, or you would rather it were summarised differently or not carried at all, that is your call and not ours — open an issue and it will be changed. Nothing on this site is intended to stand in for visiting the source, and no image or video here is hosted by us: photography and footage remain the property of whoever made them.
