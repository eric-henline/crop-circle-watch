Sources

Where this dashboard gets its information

Crop Circle Watch does not photograph or investigate formations itself — it reads the people who do, verifies the report date, and links straight back to them. These are the sources the daily scan checks, what each one actually offers, and what it is best at. Every formation entry on this site credits its source; nothing here is a substitute for visiting them.

Primary aggregators

Where a new formation is usually reported first, and the two sources most entries on this dashboard cite.

Aggregator cropcircleconnector.com

The Crop Circle Connector

The field's central clearing house, running since the 1990s. A season index for each year, with a page per formation carrying aerial photography, an OS grid reference, and often ground reports, diagrams and dowsing notes. Its per-formation URLs embed the report year, which makes it unusually easy to tell a current report from an old page resurfacing in search.

  • Season index by year
  • Map references
  • Aerial photo sets
  • Ground reports

Best at breadth and speed — usually the first to post a new formation.

Visit site
Aggregator temporarytemples.co.uk

Temporary Temples

Run by Steve and Karen Alexander, who have photographed the phenomenon since 1994. Each formation gets a project page built around their own photography, with geometric drawings, measurements and a discussion of the pattern's construction. Also books and year books, a video library, online courses and an annual conference.

  • Formation analysis
  • Geometry drawings
  • Year books
  • Conference & courses

Best at the most considered write-up per formation — typically a few days behind the first report, and worth the wait.

Visit site

Photography & field documentation

The people in the fields and in the air. Most imagery credited on this dashboard originates here.

Photography cropcirclephotographs.co.uk

Lucy Pringle

A photographer and researcher who has documented formations since 1990, giving her one of the longest continuous visual archives in the field. The site carries that archive alongside articles and newsletters, and she runs guided tours of Wiltshire formations and the sacred sites around them.

  • Archive from 1990
  • Articles & newsletter
  • Guided tours

Best at the long view — three and a half decades of continuous photographic record.

Visit site
Video youtube.com/@StonehengeDronescapes

Stonehenge Dronescapes

A commercial drone pilot who flies the Wiltshire formations between Avebury and Stonehenge each season, publishing short aerial films — often within a day or two of a formation appearing. A large share of the footage embedded on this dashboard is theirs.

  • Aerial video
  • Same-week uploads
  • Wiltshire focus

Best at seeing the pattern whole — frequently the only aerial record a formation gets before it is cut.

Visit channel

Archives & community

Historical depth, and the open forums where word of a new formation often travels first.

Archive circleresearcharchive.com

Crop Circle Research Archive

A free digital repository digitising the paper archives of researchers active from the 1980s through the early 2000s — over 600 magazines and articles so far, plus chronologies and bibliographies. Still actively being scanned and added to.

  • 600+ digitised magazines
  • Chronologies
  • Bibliographies

Best at the pre-internet record — material that exists nowhere else online.

Visit archive
Commentary thecroppie.com

The Croppie

A blog covering the British scene since 2015, and the field's most sceptical regular voice. Alongside formation write-ups it runs commentary columns, a reader Q&A with working circle makers, and debunkings of claims circulating in the community — the counterweight to sources that report the phenomenon uncritically.

  • Season commentary
  • Circle-maker interviews
  • Sceptical analysis

Best at scrutiny — the source most likely to say a formation is human-made and explain how.

Visit site
Community reddit.com/r/cropcircles

r/cropcircles

The main open discussion venue, and often where word of a new formation travels before the aggregators publish. Useful as an early signal, but posts carry no reliable date structure and reposted photo sets of old formations are common — this dashboard treats it as a lead to verify elsewhere, never as a source on its own.

  • Early word-of-mouth
  • Photo sets
  • Open discussion

Best at speed — at the cost of reliability. Always corroborate before trusting a date.

Visit subreddit
